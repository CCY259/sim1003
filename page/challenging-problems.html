Some Challenging Problems (for Advanced Learners)

NOTE! This page is for those who wants to test their skills on some difficult questions that would not come out in exam. Feel free to skip it if it does not suit you!

Problem 1. Integrals of products of polynomials and exponentials

Let $p(x)$ be a real polynomial and let $\alpha$ be a nonzero real number. Show that $$\int p(x)e^{\alpha x} = e^{\alpha x}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} (-1)^k \frac{p^{(k)}(x)}{\alpha^{k+1}}+C.$$ Note that this immediately solves a lot of questions in Tutorial 2, but it does not mean you can use it in exam!!!

Problem 2. Fibonacci power series

The Fibonacci numbers are defined by $F_0 = 0$, $F_1 = 1$ and $F_{n+2} = F_{n+1} + F_n$ for $n \geq 0$. Define $$F(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} F_n x^n.$$ Determine $F(x)$ in closed form. Hence, or otherwise, find the radius of convergence of the series.

Problem 3. A result in limits and its application to probability

(i) Let $\{a_n\}$ be a real sequence converging to $\ell$. Show that $$\lim_{n\rightarrow \infty} \left(1+\frac{a_n}{n}\right)^n=e^\ell.$$ (ii) Deduce that binomial distributions can be approximated by Poisson distributions, that is, if $\{p_n\}$ is a sequence with $p_n\rightarrow 0$ and $np_n\rightarrow \lambda$ as $n\rightarrow \infty$, where $\lambda>0$, then for $k=0,1,2,\dots,$ $$\lim_{n\rightarrow \infty}\binom{n}{k}p_n^k(1-p_n)^{n-k} = \frac{\lambda^k}{k!}e^{-\lambda}.$$

Problem 4. Unicycle Engineering

An engineer builds himself a unicycle with a square wheel of side $2a$, and wonders now what type of road surface he should make in order that he can ride his unicycle while remaining at a constant height. A mathematician friend of his says a road surface with the equation $$y = a \left( 1 - \cosh \frac{x}{a} \right)$$ would work and gives her reasoning as follows: call the road surface $(x(s), y(s))$, parametrized by arc length and without loss of generality start the middle of the base of the wheel at $(0,0)$ with the base parallel to the $x$-axis.
(i) Let's now say the square wheel has rolled distance $s$. Find the coordinates of the centre of the wheel and hence show that a rider's height will remain constant if $$y(s) - s y'(s) + a \sqrt{1 - y'(s)^2} = a.$$ (ii) By differentiating this equation with respect to $s$, or otherwise, find $y(s)$ and $x(s)$, and hence show the mathematician's answer to be correct.

Problem 5. Alternative way to describe lines

Let $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ be vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with $\mathbf{a} \neq \mathbf{0}$.
(i) If $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} \neq 0$, show that there is no point $P$ satisying $\overrightarrow{OP} \times \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{b}$.
(ii) If $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0$, show that the points $P$ satisfying $\overrightarrow{OP} \times \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{b}$ form a line passing through the point with position vector $\frac{\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{a}|^2}$ and parallel to the vector $\mathbf{a}$.
(iii) Let $\mathbf{a}_1, \mathbf{a}_2,\mathbf{b}_1, \mathbf{b}_2$ be such that $\mathbf{a}_i \cdot \mathbf{b}_i = 0$ for $i=1,2$. Let $L_1$ and $L_2$ be two nonparallel lines consist of the points $P$ satisfying the equations $\overrightarrow{OP} \times \mathbf{a}_1 = \mathbf{b}_1$ and $\overrightarrow{OP} \times \mathbf{a}_2 = \mathbf{b}_2$, respectively. For $i = 1, 2$, let $\Pi_i$ denote the plane containing $L_i$ consisting of points $P$ that satisfy an equation of the form $\overrightarrow{OP} \cdot (\mathbf{a}_1 \times \mathbf{a}_2) = c_i$. Show that $c_1 = \mathbf{b}_1 \cdot \mathbf{a}_2$ and find $c_2$. Hence show that the distance between the lines is $$ \frac{|\mathbf{a}_1 \cdot \mathbf{b}_2 + \mathbf{a}_2 \cdot \mathbf{b}_1|}{|\mathbf{a}_1 \times \mathbf{a}_2|}.$$
